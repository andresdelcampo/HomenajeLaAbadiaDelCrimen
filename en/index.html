I · The case file

A medieval Sherlock Holmes

The isometric adventure is only the doorway. Behind it runs an unforgiving timetable: while William loses his way down a corridor, monks work, conspire, move objects, report intruders, and die on schedule.

THE MISSIONThe Abbot wants a culprit before Bernard Gui arrives. He also wants the investigation to remain discreet.
THE METHODWatch routines, overhear conversations, and reach the places one is explicitly forbidden to enter.
THE RULEPrime and vespers in church; sext in the refectory; compline in the cell. Obsequium measures the limits of the Abbot’s patience.
THE DANGERA book, blackened fingers, a mirror, a dwindling lamp, and a library designed to make visitors disappear.
BOOK · CODE · ACCESS

The premise takes the frame of The Name of the Rose: a rationalist Franciscan and his young novice arrive at an Italian abbey where an impossible death heralds more. William of Occam replaces the novel’s William of Baskerville; names change, pieces are compressed, and the extensive theological debate is transformed into action, schedules, and architecture.

Menéndez and Delcán tried unsuccessfully to secure the novel’s title, so they adopted a phrase Umberto Eco had once considered as an alternative title: La Abadía del Crimen. The missing licence ultimately gave the game an identity of its own.

“Something horrible has happened.” Four words, and we are already obeying a man we should not trust.

The game was completed and began circulating late in 1987; its major press debut came in January and February 1988. That split explains why catalogues variously date it 1987, 1988, or even 1989 according to platform, publisher, and reissue.

II · The builders

Two friends raise an abbey

A programmer determined to finish something unsurpassable, and an architecture student able to turn a handful of pixels into stone, height, and perspective. Development took roughly fourteen months. [source]

Program, systems, direction

Paco Menéndez

He had programmed Fred and Sir Fred. Here he built pathfinding, isometric animation, camera priorities, inventory, doors, schedules, and every monk’s behaviour. Fitting that abbey into less than 128 KB was difficult enough; making it feel inhabited was the real flourish.

Graphic and architectural design

Juan Delcán

Paco’s friend since they were nine and then an architecture student. He planned the rooms on paper, sacrificed ornament when memory ran out, and made a railing, a shadow, and four colours suggest a coherent building.

Adaptations and versions

Paco Menéndez · José Ramón Fernández Maquieira · Opera team

Paco took the CPC original to Spectrum. Fernández Maquieira participated in the MSX adaptation, while former Indescomp colleagues at Opera helped with the PC conversion. The MSX edition remains visually close to Spectrum, although its cassette and disk releases use different palettes.

Publication and commercial life

Míster Chip · MCM · Opera Soft

The game was created outside Opera and circulated in editions bearing the Míster Chip, MCM, and Opera Soft names. The release sequence varies across platform records; it should not be confused with the rights-assignment contract signed with Opera in 1989. Members of Opera also helped complete conversions.
