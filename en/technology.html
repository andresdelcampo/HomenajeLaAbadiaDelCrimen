VIII · Five machines and a remake

The same stone, other palettes

The five contemporary versions retain the plan and recognisable sprites, while adapting colour, detail, and memory use to each machine. The final tab jumps ahead to the later VGA remake to show how far the same architecture could be carried.

4colours in the original version

The original version

Development began on the CPC 6128. Its graphics mode made it practical to design in four colours and reduce the result for other machines. This is the reference edition: complete map, architectural detail, turquoise, orange, cream, and black.

  • 128 KB and disk.
  • Full floor plan, including corridors unrelated to the plot.
  • Details such as window crosses that disappear from the 64 KB version.
  • Opening music and hardware-generated effects.
64KB forces an architectural reform

A shortened abbey

The cassette CPC 464 and disk CPC 664 could not hold the entire 6128 design. Rooms and corridors with no direct plot function were removed, and decorative elements simplified.

  • The essential mystery and required routes remain.
  • Less ornament: some windows lose their crosses.
  • Memory limits reshape both the code and the architecture.
2dominant colours per scene

Blue, yellow, and 128 K

Paco tried to fit the game into a smaller Spectrum, but only a 128 KB edition was released. Geometry survives within a two-colour presentation that makes the building more abstract and sometimes more severe.

  • No official 48 KB version.
  • Almost the same structure and central logic.
  • Occasional slowdown with several characters.
  • Reduced colour weakens depth separation without making scenes unreadable.
2palettes according to medium

Two MSX editions often confused

Fernández Maquieira participated in an adaptation whose result visually descends from Spectrum. The original cassette is black and yellow; the disk edition retains blue and yellow. That is why MSX screenshots appear to contradict one another.

  • 64 KB.
  • Graphics and plan very close to Spectrum.
  • Input MSX praised the atmosphere but criticised silence, slowdowns, and lock-ups.
  • MSX2 later received a 16-colour remaster with time-of-day palettes.
CGAcolour or mono, plus digital voice

Red, green, and a computer that sings

The PC version was rewritten for Intel architecture. It offers CGA colour and monochrome; visually harsher than CPC, but with the game’s most memorable sonic surprise.

  • A digitised Ave Maria through the internal speaker.
  • On copies detected as unofficial, it became a voice saying “pirata”.
  • Later reissues circulated without these digital blocks, so many owners never heard them.
  • The local archive preserves and reconstructs both timer-driven routines.
VGA256 colours · 32-bit adaptation

The stone, redrawn

Antonio Giner’s project came after the original conversions. It grew from a reverse-engineered reconstruction of the PC program and retained its building, schedule, and logic, while redrawing the abbey in 256 colours.

  • A VGA DOS version circulated from 2001.
  • The Windows adaptation added 32-bit execution, sound, and several languages.
  • It can also switch between VGA art and the colour or monochrome CPC graphics.
  • Its full story continues in chapter XIII, before Extensum.

IX · The invisible architecture

An abbey built on entry

Every room is assembled from a small vocabulary of parts and recipes able to raise floors, arches, stairs, columns, and walls whenever the camera enters a new area. The result has the richness of a complete illustration.

01PositionThe world cell and floor determine which view belongs here.
02RoomA compact list says which architectural pieces must be placed.
03BlocksEach piece is a parameterised recipe that can repeat, turn, and vary.
04TilesThe recipes deposit small 16 × 8 pixel drawings into depth layers.
05ScreenMasks and palette combine the layers into the visible setting.

02 · The grammar

Blocks that know how to vary

Each arch, staircase, or wall grows from a recipe able to repeat, mirror, and order tiles according to its parameters.

03 · The result

A room, constructed on entry

The room description places recipes; recipes emit tiles; layers and masks finally form the image seen by the player.

The graphics selector in the header also rebuilds these plates. PC, Spectrum, and MSX draw on their own data; CPC and VGA apply their artwork to the recovered common architecture. The geometry makes the same visual idea comparable while each machine retains its character.

X · The sound chamber

Ave Maria and the sounds of the abbey

The digitised Ave Maria—exclusive to the original PC version—is the centrepiece. The “pirata” accusation and the opening and background melodies also come from that version. The remaining tracks recreate in WAV the Amstrad CPC sound used by Vigasoco and belong specifically to that environment.

Amstrad CPC · Vigasoco

The speaker trick

The PC speaker was designed to produce simple beeps. The DOS version pushes it much further by driving the programmable timer at high speed, turning a stream of bits into a crude waveform. The result is rough, but in 1988 it sounded as if the abbey had learned to sing.

The local archive contains the digital data and two assembly reconstructions: one for the Ave Maria and one for the anti-piracy warning. Engineering notes locate both blocks inside the original executable and also allow its different opening and background melodies to be reconstructed.

The copy protection had a memorable punishment: it turned a sacred moment into an accusation.
XII · Inside the program

How the abbey thinks

The original source code was not preserved. Years of reverse engineering described the Z80 program line by line and enabled functionally equivalent modern implementations.

01

Grid and height

Each character occupies a grid cell with height. Stairs become commands to climb, descend, or advance.

02

Doors and objects

Keys alter the room-connection table. The pathfinder knows which rooms each character can reach.

03

States

Every monk is a little machine: working, warning, following, reporting, sleeping, or dying.

04

Routes

Characters calculate paths to fixed destinations. Adso adds lateral priorities to avoid blocking William.

05

Camera

If the player stands still, the view may leave William to show a significant act elsewhere.

The indiscreet camera

This last trick changes how the world feels. The camera leaves William to show Berengar, Bernard, Malachi, Severinus, or the Abbot whenever one of them is doing something important. The abbey seems to continue without us, although every cut answers to a specific program state.

The modern source gives names to ideas the original stored in registers and addresses: momentoDia, objetos, seBuscaRuta, obsequium. Reading those names reveals the rule beneath each scene.

Each monk repeats a few rules; together they create the unsettling impression that the abbey remembers.
